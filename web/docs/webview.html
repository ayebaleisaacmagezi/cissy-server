The website

Your site is the content of the app. These settings decide which pages the app will show, what happens to links that lead elsewhere, and how strict it is about security.

Allowed domains

The list of hosts the app treats as yours. It is filled in from your website address when you create the app, and a link to any host on this list opens inside the app.

Subdomains are covered automatically: example.com also allows shop.example.com and accounts.example.com. You only need to add a separate entry for a genuinely different domain - a payment provider on its own address, or a second brand.

Do not empty this list

With no allowed domains the app treats every page as external, including your own, and pushes people out into their browser. If you want an app that can go anywhere, leave your domain here and set external links to open in the app instead.

External links

What happens when someone taps a link to a host that is not on your list. Three choices:

SettingWhat happensWhen to use it
Open in browser The phone's browser opens the link. The app stays where it was. The default, and right for almost everyone. People expect an outside link to leave the app, and it keeps your app out of arguments about somebody else's content.
Stay in the app The page loads inside the app, whatever the host. When your checkout or login genuinely lives on somebody else's domain and sending people to a browser would lose their session.
Block Nothing loads, and a short message says the link was blocked. Apps for a closed audience where wandering off is not wanted at all - a school portal, an internal tool.

Links that are not web addresses always leave the app regardless of this setting. A tel: link opens the dialler, a mailto: link opens the mail app, and a map link opens maps. That is what people expect and there is no reason to intercept it.

Require HTTPS

On by default, and worth leaving on. It refuses to load anything over plain http://, which is the setting that stops an attacker on the same wifi from rewriting your pages.

Turn it off only if your site genuinely has no certificate. If that is the case, get one - they are free from Let's Encrypt, and modern browsers already warn your visitors.

JavaScript, storage and cache

  • JavaScript - on by default. Almost every site needs it; turning it off is for the rare content-only site and it will break most others.
  • DOM storage - on by default. This is where sites keep sessions, carts and preferences. Turning it off logs people out constantly.
  • Cache - on by default. Pages the phone has already loaded come back faster and cost less data.

Custom user agent

Optional. Whatever you put here is sent with every request the app makes, so your server can tell app traffic from browser traffic - useful for analytics, or for serving a slightly different layout. Leave it empty and the app sends the standard WebView user agent.

What the app adds around the page

The website is not left to fend for itself. Depending on which modules you enable in Studio, the app also handles file uploads, downloads, sharing, pull to refresh, deep links, and a native offline screen when the connection goes. Those are covered on their own pages.
