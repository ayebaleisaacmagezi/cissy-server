The offline screen

What people see when the page cannot load. This is the difference between an app that looks broken and one that looks like it knows what happened.

Why it matters more than it sounds

Without it, a phone with no signal shows the browser engine's own error - a grey page with a technical message and your website's address on it. People read that as "this app is broken", not "my connection is down", and it is the single most common thing that turns into a one-star review.

The built-in screen

On by default. It uses your theme colour, says what happened in a sentence, and offers a Try again button. It tells the two cases apart:

  • No connection at all - the phone cannot reach the network. The screen says to check the connection.
  • The site did not answer - the phone is online but your server did not respond, or answered with an error. Telling somebody to check their wifi in that situation is wrong and wastes their time.

The screen stays up until a page genuinely loads. It does not flash away the moment a retry starts, because a failed retry would show the broken page underneath for a second first.

Your own screen

You can replace it entirely with your own HTML. Paste a complete document into the editor on the Offline page and it is built into the app as an asset, so it works with no connection - which is the whole point. 

<!doctype html>
<html>
  <head><meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"></head>
  <body>
    <h1>No connection</h1>
    <p>We will be here when you are back.</p>
    <a href="app://retry">Try again</a>
  </body>
</html>
The one special address

A link to app://retry is the Try again button. The app intercepts it and reloads the page that failed. Everything else in your document is ordinary HTML and CSS.

It has to be self-contained

There is no connection when this screen is shown, so anything it fetches from the network will not arrive. No external stylesheets, no web fonts, no images from a CDN. Put the CSS in a <style> block and draw artwork as inline SVG. Keep the whole thing under 200 KB - it ships inside every copy of your app.

The Offline page has a few starter screens you can drop in and edit, and the phone preview renders your document exactly as the app will.

Turning it off

You can switch the offline screen off entirely, and the app will show the browser engine's own error page instead. There is very little reason to do this. It exists for the rare case where a site already handles its own offline state with a service worker and would rather keep control.
