Studio and navigation

Studio is where the app stops being a browser pointed at your site and starts being an app. Theme, native modules, and a bottom navigation bar that belongs to the app rather than to the page.

Theme colour

One colour, and the whole app is built from it - buttons, the active tab, highlights, the loading bar. Use your brand colour, the one your website already uses. Leave it empty and the app uses a neutral grey, which looks deliberate rather than half-finished.

This colours the app's own furniture only. Your website keeps whatever colours it has.

Modules

Each one is a piece of native behaviour wrapped around the page. Only turn on what you will use: several of them add a permission, and an app that asks for the camera without ever using it is a bad first impression and a common App Store rejection.

ModuleWhat it does
File uploadLets your site open the phone's file picker. Without it, file inputs on your pages are disabled.
Native sharingThe phone's own share sheet, so a page can be sent to WhatsApp or anywhere else.
Pull to refreshSwipe down at the top of a page to reload it.
CameraLets the site use the camera for uploads. Adds a camera permission prompt.
LocationLets the site ask where the phone is. Adds a location permission prompt.
Deep linksLinks to your domain open your app instead of the browser. See below.
Permissions come with an explanation

Camera and location both need a sentence saying what they are for, shown by iOS in the system prompt. Web2App writes a sensible one naming your app; you can replace it on the Features page. Apple rejects builds where the reason is vague, so make it specific to what your site actually does.

Native navigation

A bottom bar drawn by the app, with between two and five tabs. Each tab opens a page of your website.

Each tab keeps its own page loaded. Switching between them is instant and does not reload anything or lose your place on a long page - which is the main reason a native bar feels better than the one on your site.

What a tab can open

A page of your website. A path like /shop, or a full address. That is the whole list - a tab is a label, an icon and a link.

Keeping the right tab lit

A tab lights up when the page on screen is one of its pages. That works without configuration for the tab's own path, but people reach pages by tapping links inside your site, and those pages may belong to a different tab.

So each tab can list other paths that also light it up. An Account tab pointing at /account might also claim /profile and /orders. Press Also… on the tab to add them.

Lighting up is not switching

When someone follows a link from Home to /orders, the Account tab lights up so the bar tells the truth about where they are - but they stay in the Home tab, on the page they opened. Tapping the lit tab then takes them to that tab's own page. That is deliberate: moving them sideways mid-journey would lose the page they were reading.

If you turn this on, hide your site's own bar

Otherwise the phone shows two navigation bars stacked, costing about a fifth of the screen and offering the same destinations twice. Hiding your site's navigation covers how.

Deep links

With the Deep links module on, a link to your domain opens your app rather than the browser, when the app is installed. Somebody sends a friend a product link in a message; the friend taps it and lands on that product, inside the app.

On Android this needs one extra step on your side: a file at https://yourdomain/.well-known/assetlinks.json that names your package and your signing key's fingerprint. Without it Android cannot confirm the app and the site belong to the same person, and it will keep opening the browser. The Features page shows you the exact file to publish.

The preview

The phone in the middle of Studio loads your real website at a real handset width, so what you see is what the app will show. If it stays blank, your site is sending a header that refuses to be displayed inside another page - a security setting that affects this preview only. The built app is unaffected.
